## P2. Power Division, "Electric Vehicle Charging Guideline"
- File: powerdivision_ev_charging_guideline.pdf (+ .txt extraction)
- Issued by: Energy Efficiency and Conservation Section, Renewable Energy Wing, Power
  Division, Ministry of Power, Energy and Mineral Resources; foreword signed by the
  Secretary, dated 15.06.2022 (p. 2).
- Archive: https://web.archive.org/web/20240602094537/https://mpemr.gov.bd/assets/uploads/various/1711872073_Electric%20Vehicle%20Charging-english.pdf
- Key provisions (page numbers of the PDF):
  - p. 2 (foreword): aims to bring "the already existing charging system for 02 (two) and
    03 (three) wheeler electric vehicles under a regulatory regime".
  - Sec. 2.7 (p. 5): EV charging tariff is "determined by Bangladesh Energy Regulatory
    Commission (BERC), which can not be changed by the Charging Point Operator".
  - Sec. 2.2 (p. 4): a private station serving more than three EV owners is regarded as a
    public charging station.
  - Sec. 4.1.1-4.1.2 (p. 6): private charging uses the household tariff; with a separate
    meter and more than three EV owners the BERC battery-charging tariff may apply.
  - Sec. 5.1 (p. 6): only "BRTA authorized Two/Three Wheeler battery-driven cars" fall
    within the guideline. Sec. 5.4: anyone meeting the safety standards may install a
    three-wheeler charging station. Sec. 5.5 (p. 7): AC chargers of 1.0-1.9 kW for 2/3-wheelers.
  - Sec. 6.12.1-6.12.2 (p. 9): public-station tariff set by BERC; "Every charging point in a
    public charging station shall have a separate metering facility".
  - p. 8: failure to report to the monitoring platform can lead to suspension of tariff
    facilities, penalties and, finally, disconnection.
- Implication used in the paper: most battery three-wheelers in Bangladesh are not BRTA
  authorised (grey sources G01, G08), so their charging falls outside this guideline.

## The BERC question (R7): what can be said
The primary BERC tariff orders could not be retrieved on 2026-11-11 (site unavailable; no
archived copy found). The review therefore states: (1) we could not verify how current
orders treat system loss from the orders themselves; (2) the 2026 BERC technical committee
used a projected overall distribution loss of 7.37% for FY27 (news report, P4); (3) from
2023 to 2024 retail tariffs were revised by ministry order rather than through BERC public
hearings, so no loss-based rationale was published for those revisions (CPD, P3). This is
reported as a gap, with the news and think-tank status labelled.
